01 Introduction

Building a stronger design practice

I introduced structure to the design process, facilitated workshops that brought stakeholders together, and mentored designers to approach problems with greater clarity and confidence.

I didn’t just contribute to the design work. I helped improve the way the team did design work.

This case study shares a high level view. Internal details have been intentionally left out.

Turning discussion into shared principles

An example of a collaborative exercise I facilitated to help the team define and align on design principles.

Example principle
NameEffortless connectivity
What it means

Simplify every interaction so users can achieve their goals with minimal effort.

Example

Allow users to switch between personal and business accounts with one tap in the app.

  • A shared template. Every principle followed the same structure: a name, what it means and an example.
  • Small groups. Participants worked in pairs, drafting principles from the themes the group had discussed.
  • Ideas into outcomes. Facilitation turned the conversation into principles the team could use.

A clearer way of working

How design work moved through Vodafone's internal product teams, from brief to handoff.

01BriefRequest and context
02DesignFirst direction
03AlignmentAgree with product
04ReviewTeam critique
05IterationRefine the work
06Async reviewFeedback any time
07FeasibilityCheck with engineers
08HandoffReady to build

I shaped this around how Vodafone's internal teams actually worked: designers sitting alongside product owners and engineers, requests arriving at different sizes, and several people needing to agree before anything shipped. Not every request needed a full sprint, so the process scaled with the work, from quick amendments to new feature design. Shared templates, kickoffs and regular retrospectives kept it consistent.

Raising design quality

A design system and training that helped the team work the same way.

The system

Foundations, variables and reusable components, defined once and shared by design and development.

The components

Reusable components with their states, including right to left Arabic versions.

The training

A hands-on session on variables and Figma housekeeping, so designers could use the system with confidence.

02 The impact

Better ways of working

The impact was qualitative, and it showed up in how the team worked together.

  • A clearer design process. Shared expectations and defined touchpoints for designers and product owners.
  • Stronger collaboration. Structured workshops and alignment around design decisions.
  • A more consistent design language. A shared system, guides for designers and developers, and training.
  • More supported designers. Ongoing feedback, mentorship and knowledge sharing.

03 Looking back

How this shaped my leadership
