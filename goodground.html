Build your financial foundations, with no catch

Goodground is a free planning tool for people living in the UAE. It helps you see where you stand, set a goal like buying a home, and get an honest plan to reach it. No sign up, nothing stored, nothing to sell you. I researched, designed and launched it myself.

01 The problem

Tools built to sell, not to help

Most money tools in the UAE exist to collect your number for a broker or a bank. They show the most a lender will give you, not what you can comfortably live with. They hide real costs, like the 4% Land Department fee, and the rules expats face. And people avoid money decisions altogether because they feel judged or confused.

02 Who it is for

Busy, capable, a little anxious

The main audience is salaried UAE residents, mostly expats, aged roughly 25 to 45, who rent today and are thinking about their next milestone, most often a first home. They want a clear answer, not a lecture. A second audience is the people reading this as my work: employers, clients and hiring managers, so every line of copy had to read as credible and expert.

03 Research

Finding the gap

I ran a research sweep across six angles: mortgage tools in the UK, Ireland and Europe, tools in the US and Australia, financial health and budgeting products, the UAE and wider MENA market, real borrower pain points, and what makes a free finance tool spread. The gap was clear. Plenty of tools answer how much you can borrow. Almost none are independent, private and honest about what you should.

04 Voice

Warm, plain and slightly under claiming

The voice is a design decision as much as the layout. Short sentences and everyday words. It speaks to you, never shames, and is specific to life in the UAE. It never invents a statistic, a testimonial or a partnership, and it never recommends a lender or product. It gives guidance, not financial advice.

05 Learning

Teach it, then let people act on it

A plan is only useful if people understand why it says what it says. The research showed the same thing in different words: people do not trust tools that ask for numbers and give nothing back. So I designed a learning side next to the planner, and treated it as a product with its own job: build enough understanding that the plan feels earned, not handed down.

The hard part was not the content, it was the experience around it. Money content is easy to make long, dense and preachy. The design had to keep it light, scannable and honest, and keep the same promise as the rest of the site: no sign up, nothing saved.

Courses and lessons

The learning home and a lesson, as they look on the live site. One clear choice per course, then a calm reading page.

01  ·  Decision

Make learning part of the product, not a library

A planner tells people what to do. It cannot make them trust it. I added four short courses (money basics, saving, debt, investing) so anyone can understand the why behind a plan, and linked them from the home page and the main navigation so the next step is always one tap away.

02  ·  Decision

Five lessons, three to four minutes each

People who worry about money rarely have an hour. A fixed, small shape makes the whole course feel finishable before they start, and every lesson ends with one thing to do today so reading turns into action.

03  ·  Decision

A reading layout that always shows where you are

A sticky lesson list with read times sits beside a single comfortable text column. A progress bar shows lessons read. It only lasts while the page is open, which matches the privacy promise and means no account is ever needed.

04  ·  Decision

Earn trust in the content itself

Every lesson has the same blocks: a plain opening line, the UAE specifics, a myth worth busting, and a try this today action. Sources and the date checked sit at the bottom, because a finance page without sources is just an opinion.

06 Outcome

One system, from question to understanding

The courses sit beside the planner in the main navigation and on the home page, and the lesson pages keep a way back into planning, so learning and planning feed each other. What I would measure next: how many people open a lesson after a plan, how many finish a course in one sitting, and whether those people go on to download their plan.

07 What shipped

Free tools that run on your own device

  • A home page that leads with a goal, in light or dark mode to match your system.
  • Planning chats that run through to a finished plan: a start chat that asks your goal, then buy a home, build savings, clear debt and grow money.
  • Download your plan as a PDF, with nothing stored on a server.
  • Short learning pages, a blog, a story page and a plain language promise page.
08 Where it stands

Honest status

Goodground is live. The designs are desktop first, so the next step is a proper mobile version of every page. More tools are planned: build my savings, clear my debt, grow my money and a full money health check.
