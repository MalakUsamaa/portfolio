Klivvr  ·  BNPL

Making BNPL feel safe

Klivvr is a BNPL and money app. I designed its lending feature end to end: the application, the way it lives inside the app people already use, and the repayment experience after approval.

Role

Product Designer

Scope

Research, workshops, flows, UI, testing, developer handoff

Platform

Mobile app, iOS and Android

Product

Buy now, pay later credit

The problem

Lending is the heaviest thing you can ask of a new user: documents, eligibility, a long form. Dropped into an app with features already in place, it risked overwhelming people or getting lost.

My approach

Led the design from the analytics up. I found where users disappeared, validated the flow in prototype before committing to UI, and kept every screen quiet, clear and honest about what happens next.

The outcome

A guided application that tells people where they stand at every step, and a lending feature that reads as a natural part of the app. I owned UX for lending, onboarding and financial health through a growth period when transacting customers rose 20% and volume 50% month over month.

01 The challenge

Two problems that fed each other

The application itself. A lending application means documents and a long process. Done badly, it overwhelms people and they leave. The job was to guide users through every requirement efficiently and transparently, and to cut the perceived effort without cutting any of the checks that have to happen.

The feature around it. Klivvr already had features people knew. Adding a complex new one risked navigation problems and a worse overall experience. Lending had to feel like a natural extension of the app, using its structure and language, not a separate product bolted on.

02 Research

Starting with where people disappeared

  • Analysed user analytics to find the drop off points and the behaviour patterns behind them.
  • Audited the existing app end to end to find where the journey let people down.
  • Brought every team into the room, including on the ground sales representatives, to understand why users disengaged.
48.5%

of users were “dead on arrival”.

They downloaded the app, created an account and never did anything else. The insight that shaped the brief.

That reframed the project. Lending could not just be a good form. It had to be the thing that gave a new user a reason to come back, so the way into it mattered as much as the application.

03 Workshops and benchmarking

Listening across the company, borrowing the best

  • A cross functional workshop. Marketing, sales, customer service and engineering each sent a representative to share what they see from where they stand. Customer service and sales gave the most insightful feedback, because they work first hand with users every day.
  • Benchmarked local apps (Telda, Boubyan) and global ones (Revolut, Nubank, Chime, Cash App, KakaoBank, Grab) for onboarding, lending flows and how each one places lending in navigation.

The pattern was clear. The best products gave lending a clear home but let the rest of the app lead people to it.

04 Sketching and prototyping

Flow before pixels

I prototyped the entire journey across three iterations before polishing a single screen, with a stakeholder review between the first and second. Each round was put in front of users and stakeholders, and every change was judged against one test: does this make the next step clearer?

05 Testing

Two rounds, two different questions

  • Flow first. I validated the structure before touching the interface. Four of five participants knew exactly what to do next, which gave the team the confidence to commit to the approach.
  • Then the surface. With the flow proven, I refined the interface for clarity, so every screen asks for one thing and says what comes next.
06 Handoff

Applying

One question per screen, fields merged where they belong together. The hard part was legal copy: accurate, but dense and easy to misread. I walked legal through the reasoning behind each screen, and we reached a middle ground that stays compliant while clarity and low cognitive load remain the goal.

07 What I would say about it

Clarity is the conversion strategy

In lending, people do not drop off because the task is hard. They drop off because they cannot see how hard it is going to be. The work here was making each step small, honest and obviously worth taking: show the promise first, ask one thing at a time, say what happens next, and never let a fee appear without warning.

It is the same principle I use everywhere in regulated products. When money is involved, a calm interface is a feature.
