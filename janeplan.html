Jane Plan  ·  E-commerce  ·  UX, UI

Taking the stress out of checkout for a meal plan subscription

Redesigning Jane Plan's basket and checkout experience to reduce friction, improve reassurance and help more customers complete their purchase.

Role

Product Designer, Shoreditch Design Studio

Scope

Competitor research, user flows, wireframes, high fidelity UI, developer collaboration

Focus

Basket and checkout, subscription flow

Success criteria

Drop off and conversion rates

The problem

A confusing, overwhelming checkout caused high drop off, especially on mobile, and left the business short of sales and email data.

What I did

Researched dozens of competitors, mapped flows around four customer scenarios, starting from the client's brief and their checkout data by step and device, designed high fidelity UI and worked closely with developers.

The outcome

A calmer, more reassuring checkout and subscription flow, designed to be measured by drop off and conversion.

01 The problem

A checkout that made people give up

For people new to Jane Plan, checkout was confusing and overwhelming. Data showed high drop off at specific steps, especially on mobile. Fewer people completed their order, which cost the business sales and left it without the email data it needed to follow up.

03 Research

Dozens of competitors, one clear pattern

This was a heavy UX process. I researched dozens of competitors and e-commerce best practices, and combined them with user testing, to remove the complexity from starting a new meal subscription.

The competitor audit

Home, plan, menu and checkout screens from the market, lined up side by side to see where they agree.

Who I designed for

One persona, built from competitor research and user testing, kept every decision honest.

“I need a meal plan that fits my busy life and actually tastes good. Something I can stick to without feeling like I am missing out on real food.”

Persona
Sarah Turner
Marketing ManagerLondon, UKAge 44

Goal oriented and health conscious, Sarah is open to new approaches to improve her wellbeing. She is comfortable online and mostly shops on her laptop during work hours. She wants a user friendly site with nutritious plans, customisable options and clear information on pricing, ingredients and delivery, and meal planning that fits seamlessly into her routine.

Pain points
01Busy work and family lifeShe juggles a demanding job and a family, leaving little time for meal planning.
02Limited time for cookingHer schedule restricts her ability to cook nutritious meals.
03Little variety, little convenienceMonotonous meal options that do not fit around her day.
Goal

A healthier lifestyle and weight loss while managing a demanding job and family. She wants more energy and the confidence of fitting into her favourite clothes. The challenge is a sustainable eating plan that fits her schedule and still tastes good.

Fewer decisions per screen

A busy schedule means little time to think. Each step asks for one thing.

Guide the choice

Variety matters, but too much of it stalls people. Popular dishes come pre picked, changeable at any time.

Reassure at payment

Price, guarantee and what is included stay visible exactly where the card comes out.

07 Collaboration with developers

Closing the gap between design and build

I shared Figma files, stayed active in Slack and kept an open dialogue with engineering. That helped us solve technical constraints creatively and ship a design that worked inside the existing system.
