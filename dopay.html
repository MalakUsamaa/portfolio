01 The context

A product with a human edge, hidden behind a generic site

dopay gives employees instant access to their earned wages and gives businesses a simple way to pay salaries without cash risk or bank delays. What makes it different is the people: a dedicated onboarding team and real support.

The old site said none of that. It read like any payroll fintech, and a visitor could not tell why they should leave cash, or why dopay beats a bank.

  • No answer to the first question. The homepage never explained why switch from cash, or why dopay over a bank or another fintech.
  • A call to action that over promised. It suggested an instant sign up. The real process needs a check, paperwork and onboarding.
  • A form with no brand and no explanation. A generic HubSpot form, with a button that did not say what you were signing up for.
  • Pages that spoke to everyone. The SME, enterprise and employee pages felt the same, with no industry or person in focus.
  • Selling points buried. Crucial information sat deep in the page.
02 The strategy

Two audiences, one promise

Payers are business owners, HR and finance teams. They sign up, so they need to see a simpler way to pay, a smooth switch and support that stays. Payees are employees: full time, part time, seasonal, white collar and blue collar. They care about being paid instantly, free withdrawals and knowing where to go for help.

A large share of payees are blue collar workers. That set one rule for the whole site: checking a balance, withdrawing money or replacing a card must never feel complicated, so every page uses plain words and one clear next step.

How do we build a website that earns trust, drives engagement and shows dopay as a human, modern and secure financial partner?The question the strategy answered

Three strategic moves

Each page was checked against these.

Establish credibility early

Proof sits at the top: who trusts dopay, what is secure and how fast you go live.

Make it effortlessly clear

One idea per section, plain language, and the product shown instead of described.

Create emotional relevance

Real people and real stories, because pay is personal and trust is human.

03 The process

Planned in ten weeks, delivered page by page

I worked in phases and handed off in batches, so developers could start on approved pages while I designed the next ones. That kept the whole project moving instead of waiting for one big handoff.

  • Weeks 1 to 2. Planning and data: goals, audience, an audit of the current site.
  • Weeks 3 to 4. Visual direction and the homepage.
  • Weeks 5 to 6. Product and industry pages.
  • Weeks 7 to 8. Knowledge hub, blog and marketplace.
  • Weeks 9 to 10. Self onboarding and final QA.

Design reviews ran on fixed dates with the marketing director, the COO and the development team, so approvals never became a surprise at the end.

The project planObjectives, page list, timeline, review dates and who approves what, all kept in one living document the whole team could open.

04 The homepage

Answer the first question on the first screen

The headline is the strategy in one line: Payroll that works for real life. Under it, a promise in plain words, two clear actions, and the product itself, a balance and a card, so a payer sees what their team receives.

  • Trust before anything else. A band of well known client logos sits right under the first screen.
  • Six reasons, six short lines. 24/7 access, zero cost, instant setup, real time control, security and live in 24 hours. Each one reads in a glance.
  • Show how it works. The real payments screen with a clear Get Started and Watch Demo, so a visitor sees how little changes in their workflow.
  • People near the end. Stories from real users, then a personal walkthrough offer for anyone who wants a human first.

05 The funnel

A sign up that is honest about what happens next

The old form promised an instant sign up, then asked for paperwork. I rebuilt it as a branded flow that tells you the truth up front: this takes less than 3 minutes, no commitment, no credit card.

  • One question per step. Contact details, company type, business details, then documents.
  • Company types in plain words. Sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company and more, each with a one line explanation, so nobody guesses.
  • Documents without a dead end. Upload now or later. Each file has its own state: empty, uploading, done, with clear errors and a visible progress line.
  • A human finish. The last screen is a message from the founder and a clear note on what happens next.
  • Light and dark. The whole flow is designed in both, on web and on mobile.

06 The knowledge hub

A place to learn, not just a blog

dopay wants to be a thought leader on financial literacy, so the hub had to feel like a library built for two readers: a business owner starting out and an employee managing money.

  • Search and filters that scale. Filter by business size, role, topic and content type, with chips for what is applied and a clear empty state when nothing matches.
  • Six content types. Case studies, guides, reports, blogs, checklists and videos, each with its own colour tag.
  • Featured stories on top. A big card and a smaller one lead the page, then a steady grid.
  • Built for the phone. The same filters become bottom sheets on mobile.

07 The calculator

Let people see the saving, then ask for their email

A payer rarely believes a claim. They believe their own numbers. The calculator takes three simple inputs: number of employees, how salaries are paid today and how often. It returns three results: retention, time saved and money saved per year.

Anyone who wants the detail can ask for a full report by email, which gives dopay a warm lead and gives the visitor something worth leaving an address for. The figures in my designs are placeholders until dopay confirms the model.

08 The design system

One kit for every page

Before the pages, I built the kit, so the homepage, hub, calculator and flow all look like one product and developers can build from one source.

  • Colour with meaning. A brand purple scale with neutrals and teal, yellow, red and green for status, in light and dark, with semantic roles for primary, surface, outline, error, warning and success.
  • Components with states. Cards, inputs, buttons, menus, a mega menu, filter bars and sheets, each with default, expanded, filling and filled states on web and mobile.
  • Documented for handoff. A walkthrough of the kit for the team, plus annotations and specs on every approved page.
